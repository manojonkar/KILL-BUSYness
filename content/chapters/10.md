Mr. Kumar: “The answer most leaders don’t want to give publicly is this: we lost people. Some of them were genuinely strong performers by the metrics we had previously used to evaluate performance. What those metrics did not capture was the degree to which certain individuals were optimising for their own function, unmindful of the organisation as a whole.

Mr. Pandya: “The personnel dimension Mr. Kumar describes connects directly to how we think about recruitment now, and the shift there has been equally significant. _Moderator: “Ms. Desai, what has been your journey of the last five years?”_ 

Ms. Priya Desai: “It started with getting frustrated with the follow-up culture that our organisations, and many more organisations, had become. We seriously took on the NFR, No Follow-Up Required, doctrine. 

“Our most senior leadership team achieved NFRL certification, No Follow-Up Required Leader, within the first 8 months of committing to the standard, and they are now reliable to maintain that standard over time. The more demanding work has been extending the standard into the team and departmental layers, which we refer to as NFRT and NFRD. Some functions have genuinely absorbed the standard into their operating practice. Others are still in transition, much better than before – but not as reliable as we would wish them to be. Company-wide NFRC, No Follow-Up Required Company, is also a work in progress, and we are making good progress there.”
