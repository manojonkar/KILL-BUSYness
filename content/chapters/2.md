# **PURPOSE** 

_This is the first move of ROAR: Reflect._ 

Most organisations have goals and targets; what they rarely have is a genuine purpose, and without one, they remain transaction houses, not a legacy in the making. We are not talking about vision statements, mission statements, or any other statements. We are talking about a lived purpose that influences everything else in the organisation. 

**Human beings want their lives to matter.** 

**They want to be part of something bigger than themselves.** 

When we go beyond the survival and scarcity mindset and reflect, we can inquire into the fundamental questions:

Who am I? and What is my purpose? These are questions that can be answered by individuals and also by organisations. 

Connecting to these questions powerfully gives a new context for your work and your organisation, beyond mere operational survival. It gives you and your team the sense of ‘Building the Temple’ instead of being BUSY laying bricks. 

## **What Purpose Is Not** 

It is not a goal. It is not a destination. 

Purpose is the WHY of your game and its impact on the world. 

An organisation can hit every goal it sets and still be purposeless. It can lead its market and still be dying inside. 

Purpose answers a simple question: what would our stakeholders miss if we didn't exist? What does a purpose do? 

1. It gives people the energy to pursue what seems impossible. 

2. It brings together people who have similar intentions. 

3. It becomes the reference point for all decisions, people, projects, investments, and what to walk away from.

_Kill BUSYness_ 

4. It gives patience and perseverance for the long - sustained journey. 

5. Excellence becomes a natural part of the game." 

The purpose is the North Star, the decision framework for everything and everyone. It decides what gets priority, what gets funded, who gets hired, what gets refused, and what people will endure when the work becomes difficult. 

## **2.a A purpose makes decisions easier and faster.** 

When you are clear about what your organisation exists to do, a large number of decisions answer themselves. Instead of asking ‘What should we do?’ the question becomes ‘Does this serve our purpose?’ and suddenly the choice is clear. 

## **2.b Purpose and the BUSYness Connection** 

BUSYness is, in part, what fills the space where purpose should be. 

When people know why they're here, they tend to prioritise naturally. They make good calls about where to invest their energy. They protect what matters and let go of what doesn't. 

When purpose is absent, people default to activity. 

They fill their days with motion because motion feels better than uncertainty. Meetings, emails, and updates

## **2.d Purpose attracts the right people.** 

Purpose attracts people who feel deeply connected to what the organisation stands for and would be drawn here even if several other options were available. 

Research shows that people are 78% more likely to want to work for an organisation that leads with purpose (Porter Novelli, 2021). 

## **2.e Purpose retains people even in difficult times.** 

Every organisation goes through hard periods. In most organisations, those periods produce attrition; the good people leave because nothing is anchoring them there. 

## **2.f Unfortunately, Purpose Disappears into the Middle** 

The CEO means it. The management team is aligned. Then somewhere the purpose gets lost. It is communicated, but it doesn’t land, and if _it lands and inspires people, it gets buried under the BUSYness._ 

What arrives at the frontline is a set of KPIs and a list of tasks. The ‘ _why’_ has evaporated.

## **2.g When Purpose Disappears: The Organisational Cost** 

When senior leaders of an infrastructure giant first approached us about articulating a common purpose, the energy was genuine. “We want to align the group,” the CFO explained. “We are operating as separate kingdoms. A shared purpose could unite us.” We began the planning with optimism. 

Two months into the planning, halfway through finalising the intervention, the group chairman called a meeting. No pleasantries. He leaned forward and delivered his verdict in a voice that left no room for discussion: “Purpose does not make any difference in how business is run. We don't need this.” The intervention died that day. 

In a painful sense, he was right. 

An organisation that has been distracted from its founding purpose for a long time and is now BUSY hiding and managing the consequences of that drift finds both “purpose” and “co-creation” to be dangerous conversations. 

Twenty-two months later, the group collapsed. When the news broke, chaos followed. The damage to all stakeholders, investors, employees, vendors, and the communities that depended on their projects was catastrophic.
