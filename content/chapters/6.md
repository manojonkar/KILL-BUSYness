## **The Two Cultures: BUSY vs. High Performance** 

_“Does your organisation depend on follow-up and reminders to get things done?”_ 

If follow-up messages are a normal part of your week, if status chases are expected, if reminders are built into the workflow, if the question “did you follow up on that?” is a standard part of how things move forward, then you’re living in a BUSY culture.

## **A reminder culture is an accountability tax** 

Follow-up is not coordination; it is compensation for missing ownership. Commitments are enforced, not owned, and the organisation runs on surveillance rather than trust. 

## **6.a How Follow-Up Cultures Accumulate** 

Nobody designed a follow-up culture deliberately. It accumulated, one loose promise at a time, one missed deadline absorbed without consequence, layer on layer until chasing became the rhythm of the organisation. 

In teams where accountability is genuinely owned, followup messages are rarely needed. In teams where it isn’t, chasing people and deadlines becomes the most played game in town.

## **6.c NFR: No Follow-Up Required** 

NFR – No Follow-Up Required is the standard that separates BUSY cultures from High-Performance ones. 

No Follow-Up Required means in your organisation, a commitment made is a commitment honoured. Leaders and teams operate with such clarity, ownership, and integrity that no follow-ups are required. People will commit thoughtfully, but once committed, it will be done. In case of breakdowns, pro-active communication is the norm.

The evidence from these organisations confirms that once the leader personally models the standard, something in the organisation’s nervous system begins to change. 

Within months, the evidence from these organisations confirmed something nobody had predicted: a significant increase in teamwork and mutual respect. When accountability becomes the norm, trust follows. 

If organisations stop chasing and start being NFR, they free up eight to twelve hours per person per week. That is a full working day returned to strategic work, relationship building, deep thinking, the very things that a BUSY organisation can never find time for.

**6.d Culture Can Change** 

On 4 February 2014, Satya Nadella became CEO of Microsoft. The culture he inherited was what he called “know-it-all”, siloed departments protecting turf, leaders exhausted by internal politics, teams falling behind on every major platform shift (mobile, cloud, social). The company that had dominated personal computing was becoming irrelevant in the new world. Nadella’s first major declaration as CEO shocked many. It wasn’t a product announcement, a restructuring plan, or a financial target. He declared, “Our ability to change our culture is the leading indicator of our future success.” Culture first. The strategy would follow. 

The cultural shift from “know-it-all” to “learn-it-all” had to come first. Only then did the commercial transformation become possible. 

The numbers tell the story: Microsoft's market cap grew from $300 billion when Nadella took over to approximately $3 trillion by 2024, a tenfold increase. Revenue grew nearly 3x. The stock price grew roughly 10x.
