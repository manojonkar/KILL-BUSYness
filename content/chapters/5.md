WHAT IS YOUR BUSYNESS HIDING? 

BUSYness isn’t the problem. It is the solution, a remarkably effective, socially approved way of avoiding the discomfort of not knowing. 

Discomfort of not knowing whether your clients value you or merely tolerate you; whether your strategy is a real strategy; whether your best people are truly committed; what your competitors understand about your market that you don’t. 

These are difficult to know and costly not to know. BUSYness fills the space where that discomfort would live. 

The conversations that would surface real answers are slow conversations, and a BUSY organisation has no patience for slowness. It doesn’t have a shortage of information; it has a shortage of the stillness in which information becomes understanding.

## **5.a Version 1.0, Version 2.0, and the Discipline** 

When we work with organisations on long-term transformation, we ask them to label their current business as Version 1.0 and the business they’re trying to build as Version 2.0. 

The 1.0 business will consume forty-eight hours a day if you let it. Version 2.0 won’t happen, won’t go beyond a good idea, unless you create dedicated time for it and protect that time. 

As Dr. Otto Scharmer of MIT says in his book on Theory U, the first transformation for creating a new reality is Transforming Perception. It precedes transforming self and transforming action, and it cannot be skipped. 

This is also why ROAR begins with Reflect, not Run. Everything else in this model depends on whether this first movement is genuine. 

Theory U makes one more distinction worth holding: the difference between discussion, debate, and dialogue. In most meetings, people either perform alignment or advocate for positions. 

A BUSY organisation has plenty of discussion and debate, but almost no dialogue.

## **5.b The Gap Between You and What Your Clients Actually Think** 

Your dashboards show a lot of data. They don’t show what your clients think about you, whether they see you as a partner or a vendor. 

Most organisations mistake transaction history for relationship depth. They know what a client buys and what they pay. They don’t know what that client’s business is trying to become, what keeps their leadership team awake at night, or what would make them leave them for the competition OR sometimes in-house options. Assumptions, held long enough without examination, become beliefs. Beliefs, over time, become the walls of a box you can no longer see beyond.

## **5.c The Absence of Real Thinking**

## **5.f The Age of Your Assumptions** 

Every organisation develops assumptions about its market, its customers, its competitors, and its own capabilities. Most of these were valid once. 

The question is whether they are still valid and whether alternative perspectives exist. An organisation too busy to challenge its own assumptions ends up running a past version of itself in a market that has already moved on. The gap between those two places doesn’t stay fixed. It widens every year you don’t look. 

Hint: Ask the leadership team to spend a month studying the market, meeting key stakeholders, and surfacing contradictory data, information that challenges your current assumptions about yourself, your competition, your industry, and the future. 

## **5.g The Listening Practice** 

**Four levels of listening** 

Dr. Otto Scharmer’s work describes four levels, and most organisational conversations never get past the first.

**_Downloading,_** you already know what they’re going to say, so you listen just long enough to confirm it. This is the default mode of habitual listening without much conscious effort: reconfirming what you already believe, often while mentally composing your reply. 

**_Factual listening:_** You notice what’s actually different from what you expected. This requires setting your assumption aside long enough to register a fact that contradicts it. 

**_Empathic listening:_** You listen from inside the other person’s experience instead of your own. You stop evaluating what they’re saying and start sensing why it matters to them. 

**_Generative listening:_** You listen from a kind of stillness that lets something genuinely new emerge, something never fully articulated before the conversation began. This is rare, and it cannot be rushed.

## **5.h What We See When the Noise Stops** 

When a leadership team looks at its industry with fresh eyes, generative listening applied to data they had stopped really examining, a new world opens up. Most have plans, not a real strategy. When leaders engage with the discipline of deep strategic thinking, mapping Customer Segments, Product Portfolios, Channels, and Geographic Presence, new possibilities become real, not aspirational. 

_This is what BUSYness hides. A world that becomes visible only when the noise stops._
