## **PART ONE: THE FIRST 90 DAYS** 

The first four phases are about you and your leadership team creating the right environment in which you can build the HPO. 

**Phase 1, Educate Everyone on NFR (No Follow-Up Required)** 

_Duration: 1–2 weeks._ 

_Owner: CEO, personally delivered to the leadership team; cascaded by each leader to their own team._ 

No Follow-Up Required was defined in Chapter 6. Ensure that every single person in the organisation has heard it directly from a leader, in person, not by memo. 

Walk your leadership team through the four disciplines that make NFR real rather than aspirational: 

- a. systemic elimination of follow-up loops, 

   - a. formalised service-level agreements in place of vague dependence, 

   - b. calendar-driven accountability instead of reactive chasing, and 

   - c. thoughtful commitments, promises made only when delivery is genuinely possible. 

Have every leader run the Follow-Up Audit from Chapter 6.

**Phase 2: Implement NFR for Yourself and Your Leadership Team** 

_Duration: 30 days for the first certification cycle, then continued monthly thereafter._ 

_Owner: CEO first, then every member of the leadership team._ 

Re-look at your SLAs(Service Level Agreements) and commitments. Withdraw from those commitments and change those SLAs that are not adding value to the organisation. 

Certification as an NFR Leader (NFRL) is monthly, not a one-time badge. The standard is simple and unforgiving: every promise and every SLA was kept, and wherever a breakdown occurred, the leader was in timely, proactive communication about it before anyone had to ask. NFRL does not mean never breaking a commitment. It means either you are reliable in keeping your commitments fully, or whenever there is a breakdown, you are reliable in communicating proactively to all the concerned persons and managing the fallouts of the breakdown.

**Phase 3, Project Whitespace: Empty Your Calendar by 50%** 

_Duration: 2–3 weeks._ 

_Owner: CEO, Senior Leadership Team, and all People Managers, supported by HR._ 

Before you add a single new initiative, purpose work, strategy work, or redesign work, you must first make room for it. It is difficult to commit to new things, even if they are good ones, when you have a full calendar and are already struggling with BUSYness. 

Start with your calendar. Sort all activities into three categories: work that genuinely created value, work that existed to manage the absence of trust (status checks, chasing,  re-explaining),  and  work  that  was  simply

This phase is complete when every leader on the team has verifiably freed approximately 50% of their calendar and that time is protected for deep work on the future, culture, strategy, talent transformation, and execution.

## **PART TWO: THE BUILD** 

The first ninety days cleared up the ground. Now we create the foundation for building a purpose-driven, strategy-focused HPO (High-Performance Organisation). 

**Phase 5, Articulate or Reconnect with Purpose** 

_Duration: 4–6 weeks._ 

_Owner: CEO, supported by the Senior Leadership Team._ 

Some organisations need to discover their purpose for the first time. Others need to reconnect and deepen their relationship with an existing one. 

Use the Purpose Draft exercise from Chapter 2, now run with real data: what did the Sensing Journey reveal about what your clients actually value, what your people actually believe, and what the organisation is actually for, beneath what the mission statement says? Run the Purpose Translation Test from the same chapter across the leadership team and a cross-section of the frontline, and look honestly at where the answers diverge. 

A purpose drafted in a conference room and a purpose

This phase is complete when the leadership team has a purpose statement that defines the organisation's reason for existence and guides all organisational decisions. 

**Phase 6, Study Competitive Strategy, Build an Extraordinary One** 

_Duration: 6–8 weeks._ 

_Owner: CEO, supported by the Senior Leadership Team._ 

With purpose clarified, return to the three questions from Chapter 3: which customer segments, which of their needs, and what creates a long-term advantage no competitor can easily copy, and this time, answer them rigorously along with your core team. 

Study your competitors in-depth. Use what the Sensing Journey revealed as a starting point. 

If you are interested, use Roger Martin and A G Lafley’s 5- choice cascade from their book – Playing to Win. 

This phase is complete when the leadership team can state what their Winning Aspiration is, where they are going to play, and how they are going to win.

**Phase 7, Train the Leaders** 

_Duration: 6–8 weeks, run parallel with Phase 8._ 

_Owner: CEO, supported by HR._ 

Senior leaders need to be trained in Organisation Design and Organisation Transformation. Doing the OD work without the right preparation tends to reproduce the old structure with new labels and triggers corporate immunity to change. 

This phase is complete when the leadership team can train others in the connection between purpose, strategy, organisational structure, performance management, talent management, and every function in the organisation. 

**Phase 8, Redesign the Organisation in Sync with Strategy** 

_Duration: 8–10 weeks._ 

_Owner: CEO, with the Senior Leadership Team._ 

Structure follows Strategy. 

The job of the Strategy is to make your organisation unique in the eyes of the customer. 

The job of the organisational structure is to help you design and execute the strategy and create a HighPerformance Organisation (HPO).

**Phase 9: Redesign the Performance Management System in Sync with Strategy** 

_Duration: 6–8 weeks._ 

_Owner: CEO and HR, implemented by Business Heads._

A new structure sitting on top of an old performance system is an assured disaster. 

In this phase, individual and team performance are in sync with the corporate strategy scorecard and the departmental strategy scorecard.
