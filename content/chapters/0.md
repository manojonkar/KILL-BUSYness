## **_The job of management is to use resources smartly to get maximum output._** 

Strip away every framework produced in a hundred years of management thinking. This assumption is at the heart of most of them. Treated as obvious.

## **The Extractive Century** 

For the better part of a century, management has operated on one foundational mindset: Extract Maximum. 

Land, Capital, Technology, Machinery, Raw Material, People, Nature, Everything is a Resource. 

We call people at work ‘ _Human Resources_ ’, not sources. Resources to be allocated, used, and replaced. 

On the surface, it appears to work. Output surges. Metrics improve. Shareholders are satisfied. Beneath that surface, however, the ground is hollowing. 

The hollowing shows up first in the people. Energy quietly withdraws. Contribution narrows. Somewhere, in the privacy of their own experience, each person makes a private decision: 

**“Go through the motions. Make the right noises. Don't bring your heart into the game. Protect yourself. Survive.”** 

Then the culture cracks, followed by performance stagnation. 

People also start treating the company as a resource to fulfil their goals, nothing more.

## **The Three Silences** 


![](/img/chapters/00_Introduction_Reinventing_Management.pdf-0003-02.png)


In every organisation that has lost its way, these three silences can be felt, heard, and seen by those who are looking. 

## **The Silence of the Soul.** 

This is the deepest silence. The work doesn't inspire. The game being played isn't big enough, meaningful enough, or worthy enough to call forth the best in people. When the higher self, the part of a person that wants to contribute to something genuinely significant, finds nothing in the organisation worth answering that call, it goes quiet. The organisation loses its soul long before it loses its numbers. An Organisation with a depleted

consciousness lays a foundation for establishing BUSYness for individuals and the collective. 

## **The Silence of the Heart.** 

This is the silence of disengagement. People show up, complete their tasks, and go home, but they don't truly own what they do. When leaders don't create the conditions for genuine ownership, clarity of role, trust, and authority to decide, people default to compliance. They do what they're told to do. No more, and increasingly, less. An organisation of compliant people is an organisation running at a fraction of its real potential. 

People don't bring their hearts to work because they've learned it isn't safe. Somewhere along the way, one disappointing conversation at a time, one dismissed idea at a time, they decided to hold a part of themselves back. With that decision goes the creativity, the courage, and the care. An organisation without a **felt** purpose reinforces a need for individuals and the collective to show up with just enough contribution to survive. 

## **The Silence of the Head.** 

This is the silence of suppressed thinking. People have ideas, observations, insights, and solutions, but have learned that thinking independently isn't welcomed and sometimes punished. When leaders don't create the conditions for genuine thinking, the organisation loses its most valuable power: the collective intelligence of its

The greatest intellectual loss in any organisation is invisible: the ideas no one proposed because the culture had already ruled them out. 

Look around at your next meeting. Count the number of people contributing versus those who are physically present. That gap is what an extractive management philosophy produces. 

When leaders create the conditions for genuine thinking, real ownership, and meaningful purpose, people bring more of themselves to work than was ever asked for. That is a High-Performance Organisation in its essence, not a structure or a system, but a state of sourcefulness.

## **The Fundamental Shift** 


![](/img/chapters/00_Introduction_Reinventing_Management.pdf-0006-02.png)


Regenerative Management isn't a gentler, softer version of Extractive Management. It is a fundamentally different operating philosophy, one that honours the human spirit; one that treats people as the source of an organisation’s future, not just fuel for its present. 

When you treat a person as a resource, the question you ask is: what can I get from this person? When you treat them as a source, the question becomes: how can we collaborate and co-create? 

When Paul Polman became CEO of Unilever in 2009, he abolished quarterly earnings guidance and committed to running the company for all stakeholders, not just shareholders. Over his decade at Unilever, Polman delivered nearly 300% in shareholder returns, outperforming  competitors  and  the  broader  market.
