_This is the Assert move of ROAR. You have seen, you have owned. Now you set non-negotiable standards for yourself first, then for every level of the organisation._ 

_The organisation doesn't do what the CEO says. It does what the CEO does._ 

## **8.a The Standard the CEO Sets is the Standard the Organisation Holds.** 

Edgar Schein's research at MIT established that culture is shaped by what leaders pay attention to, measure, and react to. Not what they say but by what they _do_ , consistently, under pressure, when it would be easier to let something slide.

Schein’s core argument is that culture is ultimately created, embedded, evolved, and manipulated by leaders. When leaders can’t change culture, leaders must be changed. (Schein, 2010) 

**8.b Ego-centric vs. Eco-centric** 

**Ego-centric leaders demand compliance** . They enforce through fear. People perform when watched. Culture ends or reverts when they leave. 

**Eco-centric leaders demand excellence.** They inspire through the standards they hold first. People perform because they believe. The culture outlasts their tenure.

## **8.c The Four Non-Negotiables** 

These are critical elements of the CEO's personal promise to the organisation. Each one changes the architecture of how the organisation functions. 

## **Non-Negotiable One: No Follow-Up Required.** 

The NFR standard is detailed in Chapter 6. 

**Non-Negotiable Two: World-Class Best Practices in Every Function.** 

Every function must operate at the standard of global best practice. Not “good for our size.” What the world's best organisations have demonstrated is possible. One needs a serious effort to customize the best practices and bring them into the organisation. 

Rajiv entered the conference room for day one of his induction at a premium training company in India, expecting the usual: HR paperwork, an office tour, maybe a few PowerPoint slides. What he got instead was a shock. “Welcome to boot camp,” the training director said with a friendly smile. “For the next 21 days, you will eat, breathe, and dream of training excellence. Content mastery. Delivery technique. Participant psychology. Impact measurement. You will not stand in front of a single paying client until our certification panel says you’re ready.” Excellence wasn’t a goal there. It was the entry requirement. 

93 

_Kill BUSYness_ 

It didn’t stop after induction. Quarterly audits. Surprise observations. Ongoing re-certification. Rajiv, three years in and now one of the firm’s top trainers, still gets nervous before his quarterly review. “The day I stop being nervous,” he says, “is the day I’ve started coasting. That’s not allowed here.” Nobody on the team experienced this rigour as burdensome. It was simply normal, the price of being the best. 

That standard lived inside one function. Non-Negotiable Two demands the same rigour, deliberately, in every function, not just the one a particular leader happens to care about. 

Deferring development doesn't save money. It accumulates a competency deficit that presents its bill eventually, with interest. 

## **Non-Negotiable Three: Break the Silo.** 

Departmentalism is an organisation's cancer. 

In a Karnataka-based technology company, the weekly leadership meeting had become a battlefield. "“Sales promises timelines that we can't deliver,” snapped Arjun from Engineering. “Engineering takes three months for what should take three weeks,” shot back Meera from Sales." Operations blamed Finance. Finance blamed everybody. The CEO, Kumar, finally stood up and drew a circle on the whiteboard. “This circle is our company. We are all inside it. When you attack another department, 

94 

_Kill BUSYness_ 

you're attacking yourself.” “We're either one team, or we're nothing.”" That conversation was the beginning of a painful but necessary transformation. 

Three truths every department-level leader in the organisation must know, internalise, and live by: 

1. If you're married to the department, you're divorced from the company. 

2. Departments aren't real; they're created merely as a work-distribution mechanism. 

3. There are no successful departments in a failed organisation. 

The CEO must demand structurally supported collaboration across every function, refusing to allow territorial behaviour regardless of seniority. 

## **Non-Negotiable Four: People as Source, Not Resource.** 

In a High-Performance Organisation, the leader’s relationship with people is not transactional; it is generative. The leader creates conditions in which people co-create beyond their current capability. This is the most personal of the four non-negotiables. It begins in the leader’s own assumptions about what people are for. 

Global research confirms that organisations investing significantly in people development see 11% greater

profitability (Deloitte, 2023) and are twice as likely to be in the top quartile of financial performance. 

This effort goes beyond the traditional HR function's role. It is the CEO's personal operating standard. 

The Managing Director of an Indian multinational we worked with understood this distinction at the deepest level. He faced a situation that most leaders in his position would have handled through a combination of restructuring, new appointments, and a revised strategy document. He chose a different path: before announcing any structural change, before shifting any business model element, before redirecting a single priority, he invested eight months in developing his core leadership team. 

Not a one-day offsite. Eight months of deliberate leadership development, building the mindset, capability, and shared language that transformed a collective realisation, not an announcement. 

Only after that foundation was established did he bring the leadership team together to co-create the alignment for the fundamental changes the business needed: to its model, its structure, and its priorities for the future. 

## **8.d The Personal Standard: Share It & Live It.** 

These four non-negotiables are sustained by one personal practice that the CEO must maintain without exception. 

Start every meeting by connecting to the organisational

When the CEO applies this discipline consistently, the behaviour propagates without instruction. Leaders at every level begin testing their own proposals against purpose and strategy before bringing them forward, having internalised the filter rather than waiting to be told to apply it.
